Inside the VoiceRx rebuild

Version 1 let recordings from the same person appear in both the training and test data, which made its scores look better than they really were.

Version 2 is being rebuilt from scratch in Python. subject_split.py uses GroupShuffleSplit and StratifiedGroupKFold so every speaker’s recordings stay on one side. Version 1 used a Random Forest on 99 MFCC features, XGBoost on 22 UCI voice measures including jitter and shimmer, and an SVM on 100 spectral features; every module is being retrained and re-evaluated under the new splits.

New accuracy figures will be published once that evaluation is complete. Until then, VoiceRx is a research project, not a medical device.

01 / Product & machine learning

VoiceRx voice-based screening

Role
Builder and presenter
Status
Version 2 rebuild in progress
Shown at
PRAVAH 2026, Track 3: a 36 × 48 in poster built in code and rendered with Playwright · KRITI Initiative, IIT Kharagpur
Stack
Python, Flask, scikit-learn, XGBoost, MFCC and spectral features, HTML/CSS/JS, Figma

The question

A phone microphone is one of the most widely available sensors in India. VoiceRx asks what a short recording can reveal, early enough to send someone to a doctor.

Version 1

A Flask back end serving three models: XGBoost on 22 UCI voice measures for Parkinson’s, a Random Forest on 99 MFCC features for stress, and an SVM on 100 spectral features for cough. The front end was mobile-first and styled like a prescription pad, with a live waveform while you record.

What I learned

In version 1, recordings from the same person could land in both the training and test sets. That’s data leakage, and it makes a model look better than it is. Catching it changed how I read every result.

Version 2

  • Subject-level splits with GroupShuffleSplit and StratifiedGroupKFold, so each person’s recordings stay on one side.
  • The cough model reframed as respiratory-abnormality screening, because COVID-specific detection generalises poorly.
  • A triage-and-referral flow instead of an AI prescription, in line with India’s Telemedicine Practice Guidelines.
  • A small, controlled pilot at a school health event, planned before any wider use.

Datasets

Coswara for respiratory sounds; RAVDESS, CREMA-D, TESS and SAVEE for stress and arousal; UCI Parkinson’s; and PC-GITA, pending its maintainers’ approval.

VoiceRx is a student research project, not a medical device. New accuracy figures will be published after subject-level evaluation.

02 / Medical imaging & deep learning

Diabetic retinopathy detection from retina photos

Role
Led the project, working with an AI coding assistant
Status
Research prototype, tested at an outside hospital (October 2026)
Data
APTOS 2019 for training, DDR in later versions, and IDRiD kept aside as an outside test
Stack
Python, PyTorch, EfficientNet-B4, OpenCV, Grad-CAM, Gradio, Kaggle GPUs

The question

Diabetic retinopathy can quietly take a person’s sight, yet most of that loss can be prevented if it’s caught early. Screening needs specialists to read retina photos, and there aren’t nearly enough of them. Could a model flag the photos that need a doctor, so that many more people get checked?

What I built

A pipeline that crops each photo to the retina, evens out the lighting and scores it with an EfficientNet-B4 network. It suggests whether to refer, shows a heatmap of what drove the score and checks photo quality. The referral threshold was locked on validation data before any test result was seen, and a small web app lets anyone try it with a photo.

Results

  • On 349 unseen APTOS photos: AUC 0.983, catching 86% of the eyes that needed referral and clearing 97% of those that didn’t.
  • At an outside hospital with a different camera (516 IDRiD photos): AUC 0.949, but only 75% of referable eyes caught at the original threshold.
  • Re-tuning the threshold with that hospital’s own photos brought sensitivity back to 91%, at the cost of more false alarms.

What I learned

Adding 12,522 photos from 42 kinds of camera made the model worse at the outside hospital, not better. A rim test I built showed why: the model had learned to read the edge of the photo, a sign of which camera took it rather than of disease. Hiding the rim won back part of the loss, but the simpler APTOS-only model is still the one I’d use. More data only helps if you check what the model learns from it.

Datasets

APTOS 2019 Blindness Detection (Aravind Eye Hospital, via Kaggle); DDR (147 hospitals across China); and IDRiD (an eye clinic in Nanded, India), used only as the outside test.

This is a student research project, not a medical device. It hasn’t been tested on patients in a clinic or reviewed by any regulator.

03 / Research & writing

Nanobodies against cancer

Type
Narrative synthesis review
Status
Submitted to Nanomedicine: Nanotechnology, Biology, and Medicine (Elsevier)
Affiliation
Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalaya, Nalgonda
Package
Structured abstract, three-panel graphical abstract, Vancouver references

Why nanobodies

Nanobodies are single-domain antibody fragments, roughly a tenth the size of a conventional antibody. That small size makes them promising for reaching tumours and blocking immune checkpoints such as PD-1 and PD-L1.

What the review covers

  • Localised delivery strategies
  • Bispecific and multivalent constructs
  • Conjugation to nanoparticles
  • Gene- and RNA-based approaches

The submission

A cover letter, a structured abstract, a three-panel graphical abstract I designed, and Vancouver-style citations, submitted through Elsevier’s Editorial Manager.
